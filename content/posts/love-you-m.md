title: Tell me why avoidance is not a good grief dealing strategy

I don't know if this post is a story, an opinion, or a cry for help.

I don't usually post things like this. Usually it's conclusions to a series of thoughts. This time, I am smack-dab in the middle of those thoughts, and I am doing a conventionally recommended way of dealing with grief – writing about it.

Apparently, this method also works if you just write this as a letter and send it into the void. But that's a level of vulnerability I'm' not ready for yet. At least not today. Maybe next week.

I lost my friend **M** over five weeks ago. Three days after receiving this information - I gave myself four weeks to completely avoid thinking about it. I didn't avoid it completely, but I avoided it a fair bit.

_**M did this of their own will**_. I've tried writing this sentence in a bunch of ways - but only this version makes me not bawl my eyes out.

I had known **M** for the better part of a decade. We weren't very close, but we maintained an honest and consistent friendship. A couple days before their death, we hung out for an extended period of time. We _hung out_. **M** shared about life. Some setbacks, some pains. Some hopes, some aspirations – maybe ambitions. Some loss and some grief. I shared also shared some. Some platitudes, too. Promises were made to be more intentional and consistent in our connection.

> I don't understand why avoidance gets such a bad rep as a strategy for dealing with pain. I know the common wisdom - it's not healing; it's not moving through the pain and coming out the other side; it will come out in not-good ways; etc.
>
> Dear reader – I have not been this productive in my _entire fucking life_.

When I first learned of **M**'s death, it was without detail. Just that they were alive no more. The next couple of hours moved through loss, sadness, regret, consolation. Anger at them for being so careless. Rationalization to deal with this. From dust, to dust – haven't you heard it?

I think I have now glimpsed a kind of pain whose existence horrified me. I don't know how to deal with this. That is why - on Day 3 itself, I chose not to. [I know that grief comes in waves](https://www.reddit.com/r/Assistance/comments/hax0t/my_friend_just_died_i_dont_know_what_to_do/c1u0rx2/). The waves must also get smaller with time, right? Maybe I can meet the waves when they are smaller.

I should have done better. I wish **M** had done better. I wish the world hadn't convinced **M** to take this step. There's perhaps a world where **M** is here and better, and perhaps that world was just one conversation away – I wish I was in that world. I wish I could have seen the pain.
